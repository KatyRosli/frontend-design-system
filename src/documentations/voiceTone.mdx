# Voice and Tone Principles

A product's interface is a conversation between the user and the system.

Every message, label, button, error state, and notification communicates something about the product.

The Frontend Design System uses a voice that is:

- Clear
- Helpful
- Confident
- Inclusive
- Human

Our goal is to help users understand what is happening and what they should do next.

---

# Voice

## Our personality

The design system voice remains consistent across all experiences.

We communicate with users in a way that is:

---

# Clear

## Make information easy to understand

Use simple language and avoid unnecessary complexity.

Users should immediately understand:

- What happened
- Why it happened
- What action they can take

Avoid technical language unless it is necessary.

### Good

Your password must contain at least 8 characters.

### Avoid

Password validation requirements have not been satisfied.

---

# Helpful

## Guide users toward success

Messages should help users complete their goals.

Instead of only explaining a problem, provide guidance on how to solve it.

### Good

Your session has expired.
Please sign in again to continue.

### Avoid

Error 401.

---

# Confident

## Communicate with certainty

Use clear and direct language.

Avoid uncertain or confusing messages.

### Good

Your changes have been saved.

### Avoid

Your changes might have been saved.

---

# Human

## Design conversations, not system messages

Interfaces should feel natural and respectful.

Use language that sounds like a person helping another person.

### Good

We couldn't upload your file.
Try again or choose another file.

### Avoid

Upload operation failed.

---

# Inclusive

## Create experiences for everyone

Use language that respects all users.

Avoid:

- Assumptions about users
- Gender-specific language
- Blaming language
- Exclusionary terminology

Use:

They
Their
You
Your

when referring to users.

---

# Tone

## Adapt communication based on context

While our voice stays consistent, our tone changes depending on the user's situation.

---

# Success Messages

## Positive and encouraging

When users complete an action, confirm success clearly.

Examples:

Profile updated successfully.

Your payment has been completed.

Principles:

- Be concise
- Confirm the outcome
- Avoid unnecessary celebration

---

# Error Messages

## Helpful, not blaming

Errors should explain what happened and help users recover.

Structure:

What happened

- Why it happened (when useful)
- How to fix it

Example:

We couldn't upload your image.

The file size exceeds 10MB.
Choose a smaller file and try again.

Avoid:

You uploaded an invalid file.

---

# Empty States

## Explain and encourage action

Empty states should tell users:

1. What they are seeing
2. Why it is empty
3. What they can do next

Example:
No projects yet.

Create your first project to start collaborating with your team.

Avoid:

No data found.

---

# Loading States

## Keep users informed

Loading messages should communicate progress when needed.

Examples:

Loading your projects...

Saving changes...

Avoid:

Please wait...

without context.

---

# Buttons and Actions

## Use action-oriented language

Buttons should clearly describe what happens next.

Prefer:

Save changes
Create account
Upload file
Continue
Delete project

Avoid:

Submit
OK
Click here
Proceed

when a more specific action is possible.

---

# Writing Guidelines

## Use sentence case

Prefer:

Create new project

Avoid:

Create New Project

---

## Keep content concise

Remove unnecessary words.

Before:

Please click the button below in order to continue to the next step.

After:

Continue

---

## Use active voice

Prefer:

We saved your changes.

Avoid:

Your changes were saved by the system.

---

# Component Content Standards

Every component should consider:

## Labels

Should describe the purpose clearly.

Example:

Email address

not:

Input field

---

## Placeholder Text

Placeholders provide examples, not labels.

Good:

name@example.com

Avoid:

Enter email here

as the only label.

---

## Error Messages

Should explain:

- The problem
- The solution

Example:

Password is too short.
Use at least 8 characters.

---

# Voice Checklist

Before publishing UI content, ask:

✅ Is it clear?

✅ Is it helpful?

✅ Is it respectful?

✅ Does it tell users what to do next?

✅ Does it avoid unnecessary technical language?

✅ Does it work for all users?

---

# Our Commitment

The Frontend Design System treats content as part of the user experience.

By creating consistent voice and tone guidelines, teams can build products that feel reliable, understandable, and human.
